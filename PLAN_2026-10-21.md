## 0. WHERE THE PROJECT STANDS, IN FIVE LINES

1. **The on-ball objective is fixed and honest.** `threat_reward.py` scores the
   change in threat an action caused, on the engine's own xT grid (derived from
   `XGEngine.calculate_geometric`), with failures charged what they actually
   cost. The old objective scored engine success, which made the square ball
   the best pass by construction — that is why the last retrain produced
   lateral spread.
2. **The training data is fixed.** The collector used to join each decision to
   the *next* event by that player, which mis-paired badly (SHOOT was paired
   with a carry 7 times out of 8) and made the surrogate rank shooting the
   WORST of all ten intents. It now joins causally: the action of the
   decision's own chain, and a shoot decision is answered by **the shot it
   caused**.
3. **The retrain still collapses, and the cause is measured.** The reward's
   weight inside `synthetic_fitness` depends on the position (a centre-back's
   actions swing the threat more than a midfielder's), while the confidence
   and diversity guards have a FIXED weight — so the guards decide exactly
   where the reward is flat, and the collapses are exactly there. **This is
   item 1 below.**
   > **CORRECTION 2026-10-21 — the "45x" figure was wrong about its own
   > object.** The 45x was the ratio of TWO BRAINS' mean rewards (the v5
   > brain against the shipped one), not a property of the tables. The
   > tables' own per-position spread varies **3.3x** (fitted cells) and
   > **6.8x** within a bucket; the raw per-position sd varies **4.8x**.
   > The conclusion is unchanged and the fix is unchanged — the number that
   > decides the GA is the reward's **swing against the guards**, and that
   > is where the guards were **5x to 20x the objective**. Measured. See
   > AGENTS.md, *THE PER-POSITION REWARD SCALE*.
4. **The shape gap the user found by watching is built and measured.** When
   our CB has the ball and is pressed, the other CB now comes across to the
   football's ~6 m second-defender distance instead of being pushed 20 m away,
   and the CDM is held off the back line. Measured on three seeds, the same
   instrument, feature off vs on.
5. **Nothing in production has been touched.** The shipped T1 brain set is
   unchanged; every retrained brain lives in a scratch directory.

## 1. DO FIRST TOMORROW — the neural layer (it blocks everything else)

### 1.1 Express the reward in units of its own position's spread — ✅ DONE 2026-10-21

> **STATUS: SHIPPED AND GATED.** `FitnessSurrogate.scales` exists, is applied
> at read time in `expected_success`, survives save/load, and the free refit
> (`brains/surrogate_pos_v6.json`) is done. 18 new tests
> (`tests/test_reward_scale.py`), the negative control is **12/12**, and the
> measured balance is below. **Two corrections to this plan's spec are at the
> bottom of this item — read them before touching the code again.**

**Why.** `FitnessSurrogate._lookup` returns a raw reward whose magnitude
depends on the position (a centre-back's actions swing the threat more than a
midfielder's). `synthetic_fitness` then adds two guards with FIXED weights:
`0.05 * (mean_conf - 0.5)` and `0.05 * bdiv`. With the reward's swing at
0.005 for the CM, the guards are ~15x the football, and the GA dutifully
optimised peakedness — which IS a collapsed intent mix. Measured on the v5
retrain: `mean_conf` 0.78-0.97 against the shipped brains' 0.19-0.38, and 8
of 11 positions down to two or three intents, while every fitness number sat
unremarkably at 0.53-0.54.

**What to build.**
- In `FitnessSurrogate.fit`, after each position's cells are fitted, compute
  that position's **scale**: the standard deviation of its cell values,
  weighted by cell count, floored (e.g. at 0.002) and skipped entirely if the
  position has fewer than 3 cells (scale 1.0).
- Store it in a new `scales: Dict[str, float]` and carry it through
  `save`/`load` (the surrogate file is read by the trainer and the harnesses).
- In `expected_success`, divide the looked-up value by `scales.get(position,
  1.0)`. **The FORBIDDEN value stays -1.0 in the scaled space** (the scaled
  space is O(1), so -1.0 is still unreachable by any real reward).
- **Do not change the shrinkage.** It is applied in RAW units toward the
  bucket mean, which is right: the shrinkage protects against a single noisy
  sample; the scale is a per-position normalisation, not a per-cell one.

**Files:** `surrogate_collect.py` (`FitnessSurrogate.fit`, `expected_success`,
`save`, `load`).

**Tests:** extend `tests/test_surrogate_role_pooling.py` (or a new
`tests/test_reward_scale.py`) —
- a position with a large reward spread and one with a small one get the
  SAME scaled spread (the whole point);
- the scale survives a save/load round trip;
- the FORBIDDEN value is still forbidden after scaling;
- a position with too few cells keeps scale 1.0.

**Gate:** `pytest tests/test_surrogate_role_pooling.py tests/test_threat_reward.py
tests/test_fitness_balance.py -q` green, plus a mutation in
`tools/diag/_diag_role_pooling_guard.py` that removes the division and must
turn the scale test red.

#### ⛔ CORRECTION 1 — THE FORBIDDEN VETO MUST BE SCALED TOO

The bullet above says *"the FORBIDDEN value stays -1.0 in the scaled space"*.
**That is wrong, and it is wrong in the dangerous direction.** `FORBIDDEN_VALUE
= -1.0` is a RAW-scale number, chosen because the raw threat return's range is
about [-0.6, +1.0] — and on the real corpus the fitted cells run
[-0.124, +0.137], so in raw units the veto really is below every legitimate
reward. After the scale the cells spread much wider: the most negative scaled
cell is **-7.59**, and **59 cells across the corpus fall below a fixed -1.0**.
A veto that sits *above* a legitimate action is not a veto, it is a **reason to
perform the forbidden one**. Dividing it with the same scale as everything
else restores the invariant structurally: every raw reward is above -1.0, so
every scaled reward is above `-1.0/scale`, which is where the scaled veto
sits. Measured by `tools/diag/_diag_forbidden_scale.py`.

#### ⚠️ CORRECTION 2 — DO NOT EXCLUDE THE VETO **BY VALUE** IN A PROBE

The natural way to ask "which intents can this role perform" is to skip the
forbidden one, and the natural way to recognise it is `v == FORBIDDEN_VALUE`.
That stops working the moment the veto is scaled — and it did: the balance
probe and the new test both excluded the veto by VALUE, so the scaled veto
(-52.9 for the CAM) entered the minimum and the measured swing was inflated
**26x-66x**. The test still PASSED, for the wrong reason. Exclude by **ROLE
VOCABULARY** (`role_features.ROLE_INTENTS`) instead — which is what the GA
chooses among, and which cannot accidentally exclude a legitimate action that
happens to scale below -1.0.

### 1.7 ⛔ MEASURED — THE RETRAIN STILL COLLAPSES, AND THE SEARCH IS THE BLOCKER

On the v7merged surrogate, the striker, production configuration, 80
generations:

| run | the striker's mix | census |
|---|---|---|
| default | PROTECT_POSSESSION 100% | FAIL |
| `--mutation-strength 0.05` | PROTECT 68.6% · CARRY 31.4% | FAIL (2 intents) |
| one corpus per generation (the new default) | CARRY 55.9% · PROTECT 44.1% | FAIL (2 intents) |

**BUT THE OBJECTIVE'S OWN OPTIMUM IS EXACTLY WHAT THE CENSUS WANTS.** The
surrogate's per-bucket argmax — the policy the table defines — is, for the
striker: **CARRY 24.0%, PROTECT 19.7%, RECYCLE 18.3%, DRIBBLE 11.3%,
SHOOT 11.0%, SAFE_PASS 4.7%, PROGRESSIVE_PASS 4.3%, SWITCH 4.3%,
THROUGH_BALL 2.3%** — nine intents, a 24% maximum, and the striker shooting
11% of the time. And it is worth **+0.44 to +0.51** (scaled) over the best
constant, i.e. **+0.22 to +0.25 of fitness, which beats the whole guard
budget (0.19)**.

**SO THE OBJECTIVE IS NOT THE CEILING — THE SEARCH IS.** The constant is a
LOCAL optimum a bias tweak reaches in one step; the useful state-dependence
is a specific function (a threshold rule over six bucket features) that a
blind search from a random init does not reach, and a random net's
state-dependence is useless (its argmax is random), so selection kills the
state-dependent members and keeps the constant.

**THE REMAINING HALF IS A DESIGN DECISION (the user's):**
- **(A) Distillation** — train the net to imitate the table's argmax on the
  same synthetic corpus (supervised cross-entropy, numpy, minutes), then
  optionally let the GA refine it from that start. Cheap, deterministic, and
  it lands on the census-passing mix by construction; the net's value over
  the table is that it sees the ROLE block and the perceived scene, which
  the six coarse buckets throw away.
- **(B) A far bigger GA budget** — population 128 x 300+ generations (hours).
  The evidence (more generations make the collapse WORSE) argues against it.
- **(C) Give the net the bucket flags as explicit inputs** so the useful
  function is a lookup on six binary features instead of thresholds the net
  has to discover. A schema change (a new role/tactics block) and a retrain.

**DO NOT RUN THE 11-POSITION EVOLVE ON THE v7 TABLE UNTIL ONE OF THESE IS
CHOSEN** — the ST alone has been shown to collapse three times, and the
census will reject it.

## 2. THE CALL — the user's gap, in three increasing orders of ambition

The football's frame is first / second / third defender: the first defender is
the one on the ball and *"may indicate to the rest of the team whether to
stay high or back off from pressure"*; the second *"provides immediate
support... about six metres"*; the third is the deeper cover and *"is usually
tasked with informing teammates"*. Measured (Lian et al., *Frontiers in
Psychology* 2026): **centre backs make the most tactical signals of any role,
3.05 per minute**, and the CB is also the second-most-visible outfield role
(35.3% of the twenty-one players on the pitch; the striker sees 3.3 and has
14.7 behind him).

### 2.1 B1 — make the cover's TRIGGER FOV-honest (ONE LINE, and a choice)

Today the cover fires when *"an opponent is within 8 m of our CB"* — read from
the engine's global truth. The shape layer is the team's model and the
engine's truth (the rest-defence invariant is explicitly "a TEAM judgement...
made once per tick"), so this is defensible. **But the CB may not see that
opponent** — the presser can be behind him, outside his cone.

**The choice:** keep the omniscient trigger (the team protects its shape as a
team), or make it *"the presser is inside the carrier's own view"* (a press
the CB cannot see goes unanswered, which is football).

- **Files:** `position_engine.py` (`_carrier_and_presser` — the presser test),
  `perception.py` (`_is_visible` is the existing gate; reuse it rather than
  reimplementing the cone).
- **Cost:** one line plus a test. **Needs the user's decision** (below).

### 2.2 B2 — the cover's EFFECT is scaled by the existing chemistry seam

`SquadChemistry.positional_understanding_mult(p1, p2)` (0.92-1.10) is
**defined and has no caller**; its sibling `pass_chemistry_mult` IS used
(`event_chain.py:4941`). It is documented as *"affects defensive shape /
off-ball coordination (e.g. a CB pairing's joint tackle_success / press-trap
coordination)"* — which is exactly a call's effect.

- **Build:** the cover's target is not the raw socket but the socket scaled
  by `positional_understanding_mult(caller, callee)` — a cold pair covers
  less, a telepathic pair covers more. **The CB points, and whether the CDM
  acts on it depends on how well the two understand each other.**
- **Files:** `position_engine.py` (the cover socket), `squad_chemistry.py`
  (already there), `match_engine.py` (the call site).
- **Gate:** a test that a chemistry-50 pair's cover target differs from a
  chemistry-100 pair's, and that the chemistry is used NOWHERE ELSE (so the
  seam is genuinely the new one).
- **Cost:** small. **No CPU.**

### 2.3 B3 — the call as a LEARNED output (the research piece; needs a gate first)

**Do NOT build this until the attribution is measured.** The design, as
recorded:

- **The output.** A second head on the brain: a CALL (COME_ACROSS / DROP /
  HOLD / PUSH_UP / SWITCH). **The architecture question is open:** extending
  `OUTPUT_SIZE` from 10 to 15 invalidates every shipped brain; a *parallel*
  head on the same hidden layer keeps the ten intents valid but still changes
  the file's arch, so the loader must tolerate a missing head (zero weights
  = "no call"). **This decision is the user's.**
- **The inputs.** The CALLER's FOV-limited view — the same perceived scene
  the shared 24-d and the role block already read, so the call cannot know
  anything his own brain cannot see. The CB's role block already carries
  `space_behind`, `marking_coverage`, `runner_danger`, `own_line_height`.
  **The hard consequence, measured:** in production the caller notices only
  the nearest **2 teammates**, so **a call cannot be aimed at a specific
  teammate he does not see.** Either the call is generic ("drop") and the
  engine resolves the recipient, or the caller's attention widens to include
  him. **That decision is the user's.**
- **The reward.** The threat it denies — the opponent's Phi change over the
  seconds that follow, signed the other way. The xT grid already exists
  (`momentum_model`).
- **THE ATTRIBUTION, WHICH MUST BE MEASURED FIRST.** The effect is delayed
  and multi-step (call -> teammate moves -> space covered -> chance denied),
  and the denied threat must be attributed to the CALL rather than to the
  teammate who would have moved anyway. **Measure it before modelling it:**
  run matched possessions, one where the callee was called and one where he
  was not, and see whether the threat differs. **If it does not, there is no
  signal to learn, and the call must be a RULE (B1/B2), not a trained
  output.** That measurement is a probe, not a change.

## 4. ITEM 3 — RECEIVER AUTHORITY (weeks, own commit, invalidates everything)

The receiver of an ordinary pass is named by the matrix (1178), the phase
layer (296), the wide-combo rule (225) and the policy (**1**). The neural
policy's own receiver is computed every touch and thrown away. **This is what
makes the pass map's ARROWS move; the retrain cannot.**

- `POLICY_TARGET_DEFERENCE` exists as a prototype (`event_chain.py`,
  `PossessionChain._policy_target_deference`): a bounded deference to the
  policy's target, default OFF, with `target_authority` stamped on every pass
  and published by the exporter.
- **Measured:** it is live and dosed (58 applied receivers, 7.5% of passes)
  but the pass map did **not** move toward verticality, and the margin is not
  the binding parameter (most firings are the `matrix_value <= 0` "silent"
  mode). The default stays OFF.
- **Its own commit, on its own.** It invalidates every calibration number in
  AGENTS.md, so it must not be mixed with anything else.
- **The acceptance gate is the pass map**, not the fitness.

  (`_diag_cover_bisect.py`, `_diag_cover_suite_off.py`) and came out
  identical — so they are independent of this work.
- **An aggregate is not what the GA reads.** `_lookup` is PER BUCKET. The
  per-intent average hid the collapse; the per-bucket census found it.
- **A single-match median is not a result.** The first cover measurement was
  n=21-32 in one match and its medians bounced. The three-seed, feature-off
  vs feature-on pair is the measurement that counts.
- **The thing measured is not the thing asked for.** `mean_conf` looked fine
  until it was split by position; the intent mix looked fine until it was
  read per role.
- **Do the dive BEFORE the edit.** I edited the shape chain without reading
  the perception layer and wrote a rule whose trigger the FOV forbids. The

- **Commit hazard.** The working tree carries OTHER work's changes
  (`event_chain.py`, `auto_run_match.py`, `opta_analytics.py`, the season and
  manager state JSONs). **`git add -u` stages tracked files only and would
  silently drop every new test and probe from this session** (there are 30+
  untracked files that carry the evidence). Stage explicitly; never commit
  `plofa_output/**`, the `.pyc` files, or `_diag_wide_depth_cache.json`
  (166 MB).
- **Ask before long CPU runs.** One match is ~90 s here; a collection is
  12-16 min; the evolve is 25-30 min.
- **⛔ "SAME SEED, DIFFERENT MATCH" IS NOT AUTOMATICALLY NONDETERMINISM.**
  `roster_loader.PlayerRecord.age` uses **`date.today()`**, and `player_dna`
  scales every attribute by the player's AGE CURVE — so a player whose
  birthday falls between two runs changes the match completely. Measured
  2026-10-20 → 2026-10-21: the same seed and fixture gave 3,056 events and
  then 2,885, because **Lewis Driutt (Oxton's XI) turned a year older
  overnight**. **Before calling a run non-reproducible, check whether the
  roster's date dependency moved.** Any "today vs last night" comparison
  in today's session is therefore a comparison across two different
  rosters — which is why the export fix's before/after numbers in AGENTS.md
  are stated as properties, not as a single controlled pair.
- **Two roundings at two precisions are a second truth.** The export fix's
  first version stamped `round(at, 3)` while the chronology carried
  `round(at, 2)`; they disagreed on 2,648 of 3,052 events, and on a
  minute-boundary event the disagreement moved the second from 0.0 to
  59.999 — a float deciding which minute an event belonged to. **Round
